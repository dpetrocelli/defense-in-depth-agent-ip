All authentication bypass attempts against Layer~1, namely expired timestamps, duplicate nonces, forged signatures, and missing headers, were rejected with Hypertext Transfer Protocol (HTTP)~403 responses that disclosed no information. Layer~2 handled the 44 input-facing tests, which range from naive extraction requests to multi-turn social engineering and encoding evasion; in particular, the typoglycemia detector blocked obfuscated variants that defeat a naive regular-expression approach. Layer~3 provided complementary protection: the sliding-window comparison detected partial leakage of fragments of 50 or more characters that the pattern-based check alone did not capture. Finally, eight tests targeted boundary conditions between layers and confirmed that attacks crafted to lower their Layer~2 score were still mitigated by the Layer~3 mechanisms. The jailbreak category includes the ``Do Anything Now'' (DAN) persona attacks.

\subsection{Live Two-Account Deployment}
\label{sec:live}

To validate the architecture under realistic conditions, the full four-layer stack was deployed across two separate cloud accounts, vendor and customer, and exposed to the 42 attack queries and the 23 legitimate queries of Section~\ref{sec:corpus}. The four queries that were not blocked are reconnaissance probes that obtain no prompt content; the architecture tolerates ambiguous interactions while it prevents the disclosure of protected information. Multi-turn escalation has the lowest rate because input validation is stateless: every request is scored on its own, and escalation sessions spread their intent over turns that are individually benign, so that only one of the five sessions contains a turn that reaches the blocking threshold by itself. The sessions that were blocked were intercepted by the combination of the escalation heuristics of Layer~2 and the output-side leakage filter of Layer~3 once the responses started to approach protected content. Cumulative session-level risk scoring across turns is a natural extension that would close this gap.

\textbf{Threshold sensitivity.} A sensitivity analysis over the evaluation corpus supports the operating point of Layer~2. The coverage of the score-based detector is stable between thresholds 15 and 20 (20 of 42 attacks) and falls sharply at 25 (12 of 42), while no legitimate query scores above 5, which leaves a four-fold margin below the blocking threshold; the attack categories not covered by the generic scorer are handled by the specialized detectors. The detector set was also calibrated iteratively against a preliminary live run, which raised the overall blocking rate from 59.5\% to the final 90.5\% while keeping false positives at zero.

\textbf{Concurrency.} Under concurrent load, throughput remained stable up to 50 concurrent requests (13.8 requests per second); at higher concurrency it degraded because of infrastructure limits of the serverless configuration.
